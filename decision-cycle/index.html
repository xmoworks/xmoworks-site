XMO Decision Cycle for recurring executive portfolio decisions

One forum, one decision-preparation problem

Your PMO, transformation or value-office team is preparing a recurring executive portfolio review. Project reports describe progress, but the sponsor still needs to see which decision is required, the options, the source evidence and who owns the next action. XMO Decision Cycle is an exploratory, human-reviewed design-partner pilot focused on one such forum.

Who may qualify

The existing design-partner scope is for teams with ten or more active initiatives, an accountable executive sponsor, a named operating owner and a recurring executive portfolio decision forum. The team needs approved source material and must permit human review before executive outputs.

What a scoped pilot could cover

Agree the forum, decision responsibilities, source access and what to measure before the pilot. Using approved project reports, risks and dependencies, the proposed scope includes:

  • A source-linked executive brief showing the decision needed, options, owners and open assumptions.
  • A decision and action log recording the decision owner, disposition and follow-up owner and date.
  • A capacity and exception view using agreed source information to show constraints and questions for the forum.

Your team reviews the evidence and retains responsibility for decisions and approvals. See a fictional portfolio decision brief.

When this scope does not fit

This scope does not fit teams without a sponsor, operating owner, approved source material, human review or recurring executive forum, or portfolios with fewer than ten active initiatives. It excludes full PMO or enterprise governance redesign, benefits tracking or assurance, PPM replacement, production integrations, hands-on project delivery and autonomous executive decisions.

Fictional illustration: two initiatives need the same specialist

FICTIONAL ILLUSTRATION — all roles, records and figures below are invented. This is an authored explanation of the proposed brief format, not a customer case, delivered pilot result or output from a released product.

Forum: recurring executive review of a twelve-initiative portfolio. Decision owner: executive sponsor. Brief owner: PMO operating lead. Status: draft for human review; no decision approved.

Decision requested: choose which initiative receives priority access to the shared data specialist in the next review period.

Invented source references for this illustration

F2Initiative A plan: six specialist-days requested for launch preparation; Initiative A lead.

F4Capacity sheet: eight specialist-days available across the period; resource owner.

Capacity exception: six + five = eleven requested specialist-days against eight available: a three-day gap (F2–F4). These are effort units, not a forecast of calendar delay.

Options for the sponsor to review
OptionAllocation and unresolved condition
A — prioritize launch preparationAllocate six days to A and two to B; defer three days of B. B's lead must confirm whether its work can be split.
B — prioritize reporting changesAllocate five days to B and three to A; defer three days of A. A's lead must explain the launch dependency.
C — retain both requestsIdentify three additional specialist-days. Availability, suitability and any cost or approval remain unknown.

Assumptions and risks: F2 and F3 estimates use comparable effort units; F4 remains current; work may be divisible. Unrecorded dependencies, stale capacity or indivisible work could invalidate an allocation. No delivery date or outcome is established.

Conditional recommendation for review: consider A only if the sponsor confirms A's priority and both initiative leads validate its dependencies and the proposed split. Otherwise return the brief for clarification before allocation.

Decision/action log: sponsor — select, defer or request clarification at the forum; PMO lead — record disposition and confirmed follow-up dates; initiative leads and resource owner — validate estimates, dependencies and capacity before commitment. Follow-up dates are not yet agreed.

Benefit hypothesis — UNVERIFIED: prioritizing A might protect intended launch readiness. Who owns that business outcome, what baseline or proxy exists, and what evidence would test the hypothesis? These are questions for the sponsor to verify, not a benefits-tracking service or a claim of business value achieved.

Common questions

Do we have to migrate from Jira, Smartsheet, Power BI, Excel or another tool?

During qualification, we discuss the tools, approved reports and governance process your team already uses. PPM replacement and production integrations are outside this scope.

What would we measure in a pilot?

Before a pilot, we would agree what to measure with your team. That could include preparation time for one review or how clearly the brief records decisions needed, against an agreed baseline. A reduction in time, cost or delay is not guaranteed. Business-value ownership and baseline evidence are questions to verify, not an independent benefits-tracking or assurance service.
